Research

Research Statement

My research examines the safety of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems in high-stakes clinical settings. Its starting point is an empirical observation from my own experiments: semantic similarity, the backbone of modern retrieval, does not capture clinical safety. In a maternal health corpus I built, a query describing an obstetric emergency retrieved a routine recovery-nutrition document above emergency hemorrhage guidance. The system was "relevant" by every standard metric — and dangerously wrong.

My work turns that observation into a research programme with three connected strands: benchmark resources that make safety failures measurable, evaluation metrics that quantify them, and retrieval mechanisms designed to prevent them. The goal is retrieval evaluation that treats patient safety as a first-class property rather than a by-product of relevance.

Current and Selected Projects

MatSafe: A Safety-Annotated Benchmark for Maternal Health Retrieval

An ongoing project building a benchmark corpus for evaluating retrieval safety in maternal health. MatSafe covers five clinical topics — postpartum hemorrhage, hypertensive disorders and pre-eclampsia, sepsis, intrapartum care, and postnatal care — with a two-voice document structure that separates clinical guidelines from patient education materials. Alongside the corpus, I am developing safety-focused evaluation metrics, including unsafe@k, unsafe@1, safe_precision@k, and safety_gap, which surface retrieval failures with clinical consequences that standard relevance metrics leave invisible.

Status: corpus assembly and text extraction. Built on a modular Python pipeline (acquisition, extraction, cleaning, chunking, tagging, embedding, retrieval, evaluation) with a registry-based retrieval test harness.

Utility-Aware Retrieval with Symbolic Safety Constraints

My proposed doctoral research direction: a retrieval mechanism that learns document utility from downstream outcomes rather than similarity alone. The design draws on pheromone-inspired signals updated through Bayesian inference, combined with a neurosymbolic hard-constraint layer that encodes clinical rules directly — so that unsafe documents are excluded from top rankings by construction, not by chance.

Status: design and formalisation, building on MatSafe as the evaluation testbed.

Adaptive Game-Based Learning for Maternal and Neonatal Health

My MSc research (Distinction, Strathmore University) developed and evaluated an adaptive, game-based educational tool for maternal and neonatal health, combining language technologies with experimental evaluation of system behaviour in realistic conditions. This work produced my first-author publication at ICETT and seeded my current focus on AI safety in maternal health contexts.

First-author paper published at ICETT (Scopus-indexed), co-authored with Prof. Ismail Ateya.

Applied Systems: Healthcare Marketplace Platform

Outside my core research, I served as project manager and backend developer for DrugList, a Kenyan healthcare drug auction marketplace, including its v2.0 architecture incorporating a blockchain-based trust layer. This work grounds my research perspective in the realities of building and operating production systems in healthcare settings.

Role: project management, backend development, system documentation.
