When retrieval is "relevant" but not safe

I am an AI Research Fellow and Teaching Fellow at Strathmore University's School of Computing and Engineering in Nairobi. My research examines the safety of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems in high-stakes clinical settings, where I study a specific failure mode: retrieval that scores highly on semantic similarity while surfacing information that is clinically unsafe. My work develops safety-aware evaluation metrics and benchmark resources that make this gap measurable, with maternal health as the driving application domain.

Research interests: RAG system safety, retrieval evaluation, safety-aware information retrieval metrics, trustworthy NLP for clinical decision support, and neurosymbolic approaches to constraint-aware retrieval.

Short Bio

I hold an MSc in Information Technology (Distinction) from Strathmore University, where my research focused on the development and evaluation of AI systems for health applications. My first-author paper from this work was published at ICETT 2026 (Scopus-indexed), co-authored with Prof. Ismail Ateya.

My current research grew out of an empirical observation: standard semantic similarity metrics can rank the wrong document first in situations where the cost of that error is measured in patient harm, not relevance scores. I am building MatSafe, a safety-annotated benchmark corpus for maternal health retrieval, alongside a family of safety-focused evaluation metrics that quantify when a retrieval system's top results are unsafe rather than merely imprecise.

As a Teaching Fellow, I teach Data Structures and Algorithms (ICS 1201) across multiple class sections, and I have mentored Strathmore students into international AI summer schools. I am an Apple-Certified Instructor.

Research Snapshot

In preliminary experiments on a maternal health corpus, I found cases where a query describing an obstetric emergency retrieved a routine recovery-nutrition document above emergency hemorrhage guidance — a high-similarity, high-danger result that standard retrieval metrics do not penalise. This finding anchors my research agenda: relevance and safety are different properties, and clinical retrieval systems must be evaluated on both.

My current work has three strands: (1) MatSafe, a benchmark corpus covering five maternal health topics with a two-voice document structure separating clinical guidelines from patient education materials; (2) safety-aware evaluation metrics, including unsafe@k and safety-gap measures, that make retrieval failures with clinical consequences visible; and (3) a utility-aware retrieval mechanism with a symbolic hard-constraint layer, designed to prevent unsafe documents from reaching the top of the ranking in the first place.
